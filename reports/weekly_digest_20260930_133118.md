## 1. Central Bank Communications & Policy Tracker

### Federal Reserve Board – Regulatory and Supervisory Actions

The Federal Reserve Board concluded the week with publication of resolution plan feedback letters for 15 banking organizations on 29 September, continuing its supervisory oversight of systemically important financial institutions [News: Federal Reserve Board]. This supervisory communication represents the Board's ongoing assessment of Title I resolution plan adequacy under the Dodd-Frank framework.

On 24 September, the Board issued a significant regulatory development by requesting public comment on two proposals establishing a regulatory framework for Board-supervised payment stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act [News: Federal Reserve Board]. This marks a substantive expansion of the Federal Reserve's regulatory perimeter into digital asset infrastructure and reflects legislative efforts to integrate payment stablecoins within the prudential supervisory framework. The dual-proposal approach suggests the Board is developing comprehensive regulatory architecture addressing authorization, capital, liquidity, and operational resilience standards for this emerging class of supervised entities.

The Board also approved two bank holding company applications during the week: BancFirst Corporation on 22 September [News: Federal Reserve Board] and Peoples Bancorp Inc. on 25 September [News: Federal Reserve Board]. These approvals indicate continued consolidation activity within the regional banking sector, subject to statutory criteria including financial and managerial resources, competitive effects, and Community Reinvestment Act performance.

Additionally, the Board issued an enforcement action against a former employee of Sandy Spring Bank on 24 September [News: Federal Reserve Board], reflecting ongoing supervisory enforcement activity to maintain integrity within the regulated banking system.

### Policy Implications

The stablecoin regulatory proposals warrant close analytical attention. The Board's request for comment suggests the framework development is at a relatively early stage, with final rules potentially months away. This timeline creates regulatory uncertainty for payment stablecoin issuers and associated financial institutions. The proposals' content—particularly capital and liquidity requirements, permissible activities, and systemic risk safeguards—will determine whether the framework facilitates innovation while preserving monetary and financial stability.

The simultaneous focus on resolution planning and stablecoin regulation underscores the Board's dual mandate to preserve financial stability through traditional prudential tools while adapting the regulatory perimeter to technological innovation in payments infrastructure.

## 3. Analytical Assessment

### Financial Stability and Regulatory Architecture

The resolution plan feedback cycle for 15 banking organizations represents routine but critical supervisory dialogue. Resolution planning remains central to the post-crisis regulatory framework, and feedback quality signals supervisory priorities regarding operational complexity, cross-border coordination, and separability of critical operations.

The stablecoin regulatory proposals represent a potentially consequential structural development. Legislative authorization via the GENIUS Act to supervise payment stablecoin issuers expands the Federal Reserve's statutory responsibilities and operational scope. Key analytical considerations include:

- **Monetary transmission effects:** Widespread adoption of Fed-supervised stablecoins could alter demand for traditional bank deposits and money market instruments, with implications for monetary policy transmission and reserve demand.
  
- **Payments system architecture:** Regulated stablecoins may compete with or complement existing payment rails, including the Federal Reserve's FedNow infrastructure, affecting efficiency and structure of retail payments.

- **Financial stability nexus:** Stablecoin reserve asset composition, redemption mechanisms, and interconnectedness with banking organizations create potential channels for stress transmission, necessitating robust prudential standards.

The Board's two-proposal approach likely distinguishes between authorization/supervision frameworks and substantive prudential requirements. Public comment will be essential to calibrate standards that balance innovation incentives against systemic risk mitigation.

### Bank Consolidation Activity

The two bank holding company application approvals reflect ongoing consolidation trends among regional banking organizations. This consolidation may enhance operational efficiency and scale economies but warrants monitoring for effects on credit availability in local markets and competitive dynamics in regional banking.
