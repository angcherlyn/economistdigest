## 1. Central Bank Communications & Policy Tracker

### 1.1 Federal Reserve (United States)

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller delivered a speech on 15 September addressing the economic outlook and monetary policy stance. Governor Waller acknowledged that recent labor market cooling is "welcoming," yet emphasized that inflation remains "still too far from the 2% target" [News: Federal Reserve Board, 2026-09-15, Governor Waller Speech on Economic Outlook]. This formulation suggests policymakers are observing moderation in labor demand with some relief, but the Committee's dual mandate calculus continues to be weighted toward price stability concerns.

Critically, Governor Waller stated, "We need to see several more months of cooling before concluding we are done raising rates" [News: Federal Reserve Board, 2026-09-15, Governor Waller Speech on Economic Outlook]. This forward guidance indicates that the current policy cycle has not yet reached its terminal rate and that additional tightening remains under active consideration contingent on incoming data.

Earlier in the week, several Federal Reserve policymakers signaled that core service inflation remains sticky, suggesting rates may stay higher for longer [News: Reuters, 2026-09-11, Fed officials hint at sticky services inflation]. The persistence of services inflation—particularly in labor-intensive sectors—has become a central concern in the Committee's deliberations. Market participants have responded by resetting expectations, now pricing in a November rate hike instead of a pause [News: Reuters, 2026-09-11, Fed officials hint at sticky services inflation].

### 1.2 European Central Bank (Euro Area)

The European Central Bank Governing Council is emerging divided over the appropriate next policy move [News: Financial Times, 2026-09-14, ECB Governing Council divided on next policy move]. Internal deliberations center on whether to raise the deposit rate to a record high of 4.00% or to pause the tightening cycle [News: Financial Times, 2026-09-14, ECB Governing Council divided on next policy move]. This divergence reflects underlying tensions between those prioritizing inflation convergence and those concerned about the cumulative impact of past tightening on growth, credit conditions, and financial stability in peripheral member states.

## 3. Analytical Summary

The Federal Reserve communication strategy this week underscores a continued commitment to disinflation, with Governor Waller's remarks signaling a conditional but explicit openness to further rate increases. The labor market cooling cited by Waller is consistent with the August unemployment rate of 4.1% [FRED: UNRATE], a modest rise from earlier in the year. However, the stickiness of core services inflation—flagged by multiple policymakers—suggests that the disinflationary impulse from goods and energy may not be sufficient to anchor overall inflation expectations near target.

The repricing of market expectations toward a November hike reflects a broader reassessment of the policy path in light of persistent inflation dynamics. With the effective federal funds rate at 3.63% [FRED: FEDFUNDS] as of August, the current stance may be viewed by the Committee as insufficiently restrictive given the inflation shortfall relative to the 2% objective.

Across the Atlantic, the ECB's internal division highlights the growing challenge facing central banks operating in heterogeneous currency unions. The debate over whether to extend tightening to 4.00% or pause reflects differing assessments of inflation persistence, output gap estimates, and financial stability risks. The outcome of this deliberation will have significant implications for euro area monetary conditions and cross-border capital flows.
