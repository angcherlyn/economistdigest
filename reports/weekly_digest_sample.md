## 1. Central Bank Communications & Policy Tracker

### Federal Reserve System

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller delivered remarks on the economic outlook on September 15, signaling continued vigilance on inflation despite recent labor market moderation [News: Federal Reserve Board, 2026-09-15, Governor Waller Speech on Economic Outlook]. Governor Waller characterized recent labor market cooling as "welcoming" but emphasized that inflation remains "too far from the 2% target" [News: Federal Reserve Board, 2026-09-15, Governor Waller Speech on Economic Outlook]. He noted that the Committee requires "several more months of cooling before concluding we are done raising rates" [News: Federal Reserve Board, 2026-09-15, Governor Waller Speech on Economic Outlook].

The hawkish tilt in Fed communications extends beyond Governor Waller. Multiple Federal Reserve policymakers have signaled concerns regarding persistent core services inflation, with officials indicating that policy rates may need to remain elevated for an extended period [News: Reuters, 2026-09-11, Fed officials hint at sticky services inflation]. Market pricing has responded accordingly, with participants now assigning higher probability to a November rate hike rather than a pause [News: Reuters, 2026-09-11, Fed officials hint at sticky services inflation].

The emphasis on core services inflation persistence suggests the Federal Open Market Committee is reassessing the pace and extent of progress toward the inflation objective, particularly in non-goods categories where disinflation has proven more elusive.

### European Central Bank

The ECB Governing Council exhibits internal division regarding the appropriate next policy step, according to reporting from September 14 [News: Financial Times, 2026-09-14, ECB Governing Council divided on next policy move]. Policymakers are reportedly split between advocating for an additional 25 basis point increase in the deposit rate to a record high of 4.00% versus maintaining the current stance [News: Financial Times, 2026-09-14, ECB Governing Council divided on next policy move]. This divergence reflects differing assessments of inflation persistence and growth vulnerability within the euro area, and suggests heightened uncertainty regarding the terminal rate trajectory.

**Analytical Notes:**

The effective federal funds rate stood at 3.63% as of August 1 [FRED: FEDFUNDS], reflecting the Federal Reserve's cumulative tightening cycle. The unemployment rate registered 4.1% [FRED: UNRATE], consistent with Governor Waller's characterization of labor market cooling but still indicative of relatively tight labor market conditions by historical standards. The Consumer Price Index reached 334.131 [FRED: CPIAUCSL], representing continued elevation in the price level.

The juxtaposition of moderating labor market conditions with persistent concerns about services inflation highlights the dual challenge facing policymakers: achieving sufficient demand moderation to durably return inflation to target without precipitating undue labor market deterioration. The lag between the August data vintage and mid-September policy communications suggests policymakers possess more recent information reinforcing their cautious stance on declaring victory over inflation.
